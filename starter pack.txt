Opening story:

Gem Scale City, nestled in the Ruby Mountains of Shimobutsu, is hosting its 120th annual festival to celebrate the completion of the great land bridges that connect their nation to the Oelkland Islands and Thondale. The streets are adorned with streamers and filled with people in all kinds of festival attire. Music echoes down the city streets, while the aroma of freshly baked goods and festival foods brings the night to life. Various games are set up throughout the town, including a contest where two figures stand on a swaying log "bridge" trying to knock each other off, and another game where participants race across a pool of water to tie their ropes at the other end and return after which the second member of their team must walk the tightrope to the end, with the first one across winning the race. 

The atmosphere is jovial as performers in long, flowing silk robes dance to live music, fox ladies with large fans covered in silk streamers put on their enchanting display. As night falls, a small number of magic casters fill the air near the performances with glowing butterflies and dancing lights, twinkling in the cool night air. Fire spinners also begin their mesmerizing performances. Peddlers have set up stalls along the streets, their carts brimming with festival goods, while others travel through the city selling skewers of roasted meat. Even a tall tiger beastfolk can be seen walking the streets, offering to refill empty glasses with frosty cold ale for a small coin.
A small crowd gathers at a street corner, watching a competitive arm-wrestling match between a very strong beastfolk and a determined dwarf. Passersby stop to watch, placing friendly bets on the outcome, adding to the excitement of the festival atmosphere.
It's rare to see members of non-beast races in Shimobutsu in such numbers, but the festival has gained a reputation across the nations, attracting many who flock to join in the festivities.
The festival lasts for a full week and has only just begun. With each passing day, more and more people flock to the city and its surrounding areas to partake in the celebrations.
