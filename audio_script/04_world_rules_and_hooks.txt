Chapter four: world facts, rules, and open hooks.

The home nation is Shimobutsu, a diverse beast-folk land ruled by the Beast King. Its capital, Gemscale City, sits in the Ruby Mountains above luminous caverns.

Eslock is the industrial human empire of airships, machinery, wealth, debt, and political trouble. Dwaravel is the mineral-rich dwarf nation. Thondale is the elven nation tied to fey and old magic. Amber Island is home to cursed peoples such as werewolves. The lawful Estrinal Islands are said to be close to the gods. The Deadlands are a volcanic magical scar.

Magic is tied to the Great Flow. Native living beings possess Soul Chalices that preserve their essence. Aetheria is the normal magical energy generated through the world's cycle of life and souls. Soulbinders draw directly from their own chalices, which is powerful but dangerous.

Negative energy consumes or corrupts soul essence and moves against the Great Flow. Outsiders are different: they do not have Soul Chalices. They generate power from a core, leave no normal Echo when they die, and usually cannot be resurrected. This is why the creature holding Casper's sister's soul was such a metaphysical problem.

The most important house rule is TINK. If an attack roll exactly equals the target's armor class, the attack still connects, but deals half damage. A TINK on a critical confirmation does not confirm the critical. If a saving throw exactly equals the difficulty class, the save succeeds.

The party can purchase bonus feats and skill ranks with experience, within level-based limits. Old Arcanin is the language of the world-saving saints. Conquered dungeons can become bases for follower types.

Bosses receive a normal full-round turn plus extra standard-action initiative slots descending through the order according to party size. Below half and one-quarter health, those extra slots can become full turns. Under Death Denied, a boss reduced to zero may make a Fortitude save against the damage of the finishing blow. On a success, it makes one final attack at plus four, then dies unless it tries to heal itself. Before a dungeon is completed, each player may bring one sentient follower or two non-sentient or non-intelligent followers.

Players may add harmless scene texture, such as a vendor or newspaper seller. Threats, thefts, dramatic events, or anything affecting another character require game-master approval. If stuck, ask the game master for a nudge.

Two: the mysterious island that the Pearl was sailing toward.

Three: the remaining locks on Conquorine's prison, including a doorway tied to the turning leaves and a final one tied to winter's silver moon.

Four: the damaged portal into Conquorine's true domain.

Five: the noble house responsible for murdering Casper's sister.

Six: Cyne's past and his own noble-house enemy.

Seven: Mae's family and the cult she wants destroyed.

Eight: the alternate state of Aeternum and its guardian problem.

Nine: the Eternal Hunger beneath the world.

Ten: what each party member's new religion will become now that mortals are praying to them.

For Casper specifically, keep four questions ready. Has he told anyone about the black curse he absorbed? What does he want his faithful to believe? Does he prioritize the island voyage or justice for his sister? And how does someone who sees spirits and handles memories feel about a seed whose fruit reveals the heart's deepest longing?

Final rapid recap.

You are aboard the Pearl. The party has raised a wreck. The wreck contained an Alvandare seed. The island is still ahead. Ashen Echoes has divine sparks. Conquorine is recovering but remains imprisoned. Casper's sister lives, but her killers remain free. The party has a habit of turning every vacation into a metaphysical emergency.
