As each creature lives, their Lifestream Chalice fills with shimmering energy, growing richer and more vibrant with each experience, emotion, and action. This sacred energy sustains them throughout their life.

At the moment of death, the essence stored within their Lifestream Chalice flows back into the Great Flow of Life, an endless current of energy that binds all things on Matromia. Left behind is an echo—a fragile remnant of the departed, comprised of memories, feelings, and experiences, like the hollow shell of the soul.

Meanwhile, the true essence of the departed rejoins the Sea of Souls, a vast reservoir of pure energy within Matromia. This soul essence feeds the planet's lifeblood and generates Aetheria, the mystical force that flows through the world, breathing life and magic into every corner of Matromia.

The more essence present within the Sea of Souls, the stronger Matromia’s Aetheria becomes, enriching its ecosystems and empowering its magic.

When a living being passes on but chooses not to seek solace in Vynara, the realm of eternal rest, and is not claimed by their deity, their Echo becomes stranded on the Material Plane. These forsaken echoes, detached from the cycle of life and death, wander aimlessly across the surface of the world as intangible spirits. While their presence lingers, they exist in a state of isolation, unable to interact with the living directly untill there echo eventually fades.

Devoid of a Soul Chalice, these spirits lack the ability to generate their own Aetheria, the vital energy that fuels magic and life. Instead, they can only sustain themselves by drawing from the ambient Aetheria present in their surroundings. However, for most, this offers little more than the energy needed to persist in their ghostly state.

Some souls, who in life possessed exceptional mastery over Aetheria, may retain fragments of their control over the energy. These rare spirits are able to manipulate the surrounding Aetheria to a degree, granting them limited interaction with the physical world. Such occurrences are uncommon but can manifest in subtle ways—objects shifting, faint whispers, or flashes of ethereal light. These echoes represent a haunting reminder of lives unfulfilled, wandering between the realms of existence and the void.

Spellcasters tap into this vast wellspring of Aetheria to weave their spells, heal others, and summon extraordinary phenomena. All forms of magic, whether simple or grand, are born from the flow of Aetheria. Yet some gifted individuals, known as Soulbinders, have discovered how to draw upon the Aetheria within their own Lifestream Chalice, allowing them to perform magic without disrupting the planet's natural flow. Though powerful, this ability comes with risks, as overuse can deplete their chalice or fracture their soul.

By contrast, Negative Energy is a dark and corrupt form of magic that bypasses Aetheria entirely, instead consuming raw soul essence directly from the Lifestream Chalices of the living such as in the case of soulbinders, or from Matromia's own vitality. Negative Energy defies the natural order and destabilizes the Great Flow, making its use a universally condemned act. When Negative Energy is used to animate the undead, it forcibly imbues lifeless forms with stolen or corrupted essence, creating abominations that disrupt the cycle of life and death. for Soulbinders utilizing the energy of ones own soul is not considered an evil act though it is dangerous and not to be taken lightly.

Because Negative Energy flows against the natural rhythm of the Great Flow, it cannot be used to heal the living. The essence of the living harmonizes with Aetheria, and when exposed to the chaotic discord of Negative Energy, the two forces cancel each other out. Rare and enigmatic individuals, known as Shadow Weavers, have learned to stagnate the flow of their own Lifestream Chalices, enabling them to wield both Positive and Negative Energy. However, this precarious balance comes at great cost, leaving their chalices fragile and their connection to the Great Flow unstable.

Those apart from the flow

Outsiders are unique beings, fundamentally distinct from the inhabitants of Matromia. Unlike native creatures, they lack a Soul Chalice, the vessel that holds and preserves the essence of life. Instead, Outsiders generate a modest but steady flow of Aetheria directly from their core, an enigmatic source of energy that sustains them throughout their existence.

However, this absence of a Soul Chalice comes with profound consequences. Upon death, Outsiders do not leave behind an Echo, the ethereal remnant of memories, emotions, and experiences that is typically recalled to a vessel during resurrection. Without this Echo, there is no tether or essence to recall, no anchor to draw their being back to the mortal plane. As a result, Outsiders cannot be revived or resurrected through conventional means, for there is nothing left of their essence to reclaim or reintegrate into a new vessel.

This intrinsic difference in their nature sets Outsiders apart, both in life and in death, making them enigmatic figures within the balance of Matromia’s cycle of life, death, and renewal.

The Void of space between worlds

In the vast, desolate void of space, the natural connections that anchor magic to the fabric of life are severed. There is no link to the Aether that flows through a planet’s ley lines, nor to the Sea of Souls, the ethereal realm where the essence of life converges and cycles. This isolation creates a realm where conventional sources of magic are inaccessible, leaving travelers adrift in an expanse devoid of the usual fonts of arcane power.

Within this void, the only magic that can be drawn upon comes from personal or divine sources. A mage must rely entirely on the reserves held within their Soul Chalice, the vessel of their own magical essence. Elementals, in turn, depend on the energy housed in their Primordial Core, which serves as the wellspring of their existence. For those bound by faith, the only magic available is the Aether gifted to them directly by their deity—bestowed as a sacred boon and carried within as a testament to divine connection.

Thus, in the endless and unforgiving expanse of space, magic becomes a deeply personal endeavor. Survival hinges on the strength of one's internal reserves or the grace of divine favor, highlighting the fragility and resilience of life beyond the boundaries of familiar worlds. 
For those unfortunate enough to perish within the unyielding void of space, a grim fate awaits. Without the divine intervention of a god to retrieve their essence, their Echoes—the lingering imprint of their soul—are lost to the infinite expanse. This isolation severs them from the cycle of life and death, leaving no tether to the Sea of Souls or any means of restoration.

Devoid of a connection to the Aether or divine grace, these Echoes are scattered and dissolved into the emptiness, beyond the reach of mortal or magical means. Without an Echo to guide their essence back to a vessel, the fallen are rendered irretrievable, unable to be revived through conventional methods. Such a fate serves as a stark reminder of the relentless, unforgiving nature of the void, where survival hinges on faith, fortitude, or the strength of one's Soul Chalice.

The echoes of the souls they hold can never return, their lifeblood trapped in eternal stasis. Before the Great Breaking and her tragic corruption by Amorphian, Conquorine was revered as a radiant and indomitable goddess, embodying strength, unity, and leadership. As a war god, she upheld the principles of valor and honor, championing the cause of the strong—not simply those strong in power, but those strong in conviction, spirit, and purpose. Her essence was one of fierce guidance, both on and off the battlefield.

Conquorine was known as the Patron of Beast Folk She was depicted as a majestic figure, blending divine elegance with primal strength. Often portrayed with aspects resembling the beast folk themselves. Beast folk tribes saw her as their guiding star, calling upon her for wisdom in times of conflict and nurture in times of peace. Her connection to nature and the untamed wild allowed her to serve as a bridge between civilization and the primal forces of the world.

Conquorine’s Ideals and Leadership
As a war god, Conquorine believed that war was not merely an act of destruction but a crucible for forging unity and strength. She taught her followers that true warriors did not seek dominance, but rather balance and protection for their people. Her teachings emphasized the importance of strategy, understanding one's adversary, and never waging war in vain or for selfish pursuits.

As a leader, she inspired with her indomitable presence, calling forth loyalty and reverence through her unwavering sense of duty. Conquorine encouraged the beast folk to embrace their duality—their raw, primal instincts tempered by their ability to create, build, and form lasting bonds. Her followers believed that to stand strong in the face of adversity and carry the burdens of leadership was to honor Conquorine’s divine legacy.

Connection to the Wild and the Beast Folk
Conquorine was also the guardian of the natural world, revered as the goddess who balanced its ferocity with its beauty. Beast folk believed that her blessings shaped their lands, providing fertile ground, safe havens, and abundant wildlife. Her influence was reflected in their rituals, festivals, and trials of strength, all designed to honor her and the untamed spirit she represented.

It was said that Conquorine could take on the form of any beast—whether soaring with the grace of an eagle, prowling with the power of a panther, or rumbling with the force of a bear. This ability symbolized her connection with the beast folk and her role as their protector. Tribal leaders often adorned themselves with symbols of Conquorine’s beasts, invoking her favor in times of need.

Her Legacy Before the Breaking
Before the Great Breaking, Conquorine was a beacon of hope for the beast folk, guiding them to flourish as one of the most united and harmonious societies in Matromia. Her teachings and leadership were deeply rooted in the importance of strength tempered by compassion, power balanced by wisdom, and individuality unified by a shared purpose. She represented the courage to fight for what was right and the wisdom to protect what was sacred.

Her fall and eventual corruption left an indelible mark on her followers, shaping the beast folk’s history and culture for generations to come. Even in her absence, Conquorine’s ideals endure, remembered in songs, carvings, and stories passed down among the tribes. Her voice, faint and filtered through her prison, still resonates with those who remain loyal to her teachings, encouraging them to forge ahead and uphold her legacy despite the shadows looming over Matromia.
As Conquorine began to turn her back on humanity and the other races, her disdain for their rejection of the natural order—particularly the principle of survival of the fittest—grew into outright scorn. Humanity, with its tendency to rely on societal constructs and collective dependency rather than individual strength, became the symbol of this perceived weakness. Conquorine’s divine essence, once radiant and empowering, began to exude an air of judgment and resentment that seeped into her most devoted followers.

The Shift in Faith
The humans and other races who remained loyal to Conquorine were acutely aware of her changing demeanor. Many denounced their heritage, viewing their origins as flawed reflections of the goddess’s ideals. They sought to evolve, transforming themselves into beings that better aligned with her vision of strength, instinct, and primal superiority. In their minds, adopting beast-like traits was not just an act of devotion but a step toward transcending their perceived weakness and achieving the raw power that Conquorine revered.

The Divine Curse
As Conquorine’s corruption deepened, her disdain became an unintentional catalyst for change. Her divine energy, tainted by Amorphian’s influence, spilled into her followers’ souls, twisting their forms into monstrous hybrids of man and beast. Some viewed these transformations as blessings—a mark of their goddess’s favor and acknowledgment of their efforts to embody her ideals. Others saw them as a curse, fearing the madness that crept into their minds alongside their physical changes.

The curse manifested in a variety of ways:

Physical Alterations: Her followers gained beast-like features, such as fur-covered skin, claws, fangs, and predatory eyes. Their forms reflected Conquorine’s own hybrid appearance, which blended wolf, panther, and bear traits.

Enhanced Abilities: They became faster, stronger, and more resilient, better suited to survive in Matromia’s harsh and fractured world.

Mental Changes: Along with these abilities came a heightened primal instinct and aggression, making them fierce warriors but often driving them toward erratic and violent behavior.

Abandoning Humanity
The cursed races renounced their human heritage entirely, embracing their new forms as symbols of strength and devotion to Conquorine. Their societal structures shifted to reflect the goddess’s ideals, focusing on hierarchy determined by skill, strength, and prowess rather than inherited status or wealth. Communities evolved into clans and packs, bound by loyalty to their kin and to the goddess’s teachings.

However, as Conquorine’s corruption worsened, her once-noble ideals of strength began to twist into harsher, unbalanced doctrines. Her followers adapted to this darker philosophy, often eliminating the weak and viewing compassion as a betrayal of the natural order. The cursed races became isolated from other societies, forming tight-knit groups that viewed outsiders with suspicion, if not outright hostility.

Legacy of the Cursed
The cursed races came to see themselves as Conquorine’s chosen children—superior beings destined to inherit the mantle of leadership over Matromia’s fractured lands. While they still revered her as their goddess, many grappled with the instability caused by her corruption. Some even began to seek ways to purify her essence, hoping to restore her to her former glory and regain the harmony they had known before the Breaking.

In the end, the cursed races became a reflection of Conquorine herself—embodiments of strength, resilience, and primal instinct, but scarred by the darkness that now permeated their goddess.

The Magic Tree of Alvandare
They say the first time the Magic Tree of Alvandare sprouted, the world held its breath.

It rose from the soil like a slow exhale, its bark pale as moonlit water and smooth enough to reflect the stars. Even as a sapling, it shimmered faintly, as though light lived inside it and was trying—politely—to escape. Travelers who passed near it claimed they felt something tug at their memories, a gentle pull like a forgotten lullaby.

By the time it reached its full height, the tree had become a wonder spoken of in every kingdom. Its branches stretched wide and graceful, each one heavy with fruit that should not have existed side by side. A single bough might hold apples, figs, star‑pears, and fruits no tongue had ever named. Another might bear a single glowing orb that tasted like a childhood joy long buried. The tree did not choose at random; it listened. It grew what the heart longed for most.

Children who approached it found sweet berries that tasted like warm bread and safety. Warriors tasted the feast they dreamed of sharing after their final battle. The lonely tasted companionship. The grieving tasted a moment they wished they could hold again.

But the fruit was only half the miracle.

The wood of Alvandare was alive in a way no other tree dared to be. When a branch fell naturally—never cut, for no blade could bite it—it remained warm, pulsing faintly like a heartbeat. Carvers who worked with it said the grain shifted under their tools, guiding their hands toward the shape it wanted to become. A staff carved from its limbs might glow softly when lies were spoken. A flute might call the wind like an old friend. A box made from its heartwood could hold dreams the way others hold trinkets.

Even after shaping, the wood remembered. A cracked handle would mend itself overnight. A warped bow would straighten in the moonlight. Some pieces whispered to their owners in quiet moments, offering comfort or warning.

And beneath the tree, the earth was always soft, always warm, always humming with a low, soothing tone—like the world itself was singing.
