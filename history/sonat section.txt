The soft glow of the sanctuary’s magical runes pulsed gently along the walls, casting shifting patterns of light across the quiet room. The single bed sat untouched, though Mary and Jane lingered near its edge, their thoughts too tangled for rest.

Jane sat beside her, arms crossed, expression rigid. “I know.” There was a tension in her voice—one that wasn’t just frustration but something harder to place.

Jane exhaled sharply. “He’s angry,” she stated, though it felt more like an excuse than a reassurance. “He always gets like this when something doesn’t sit right with him.”

Jane’s jaw clenched, her gaze flickering toward the pendant in Mary’s hands. “Then we lose our tether,” she admitted, voice quieter than before. “We lose our ability to keep searching for her.”

Jane shook her head, standing abruptly. “Never mind.”

Mary didn’t press her further, but the weight of Jane’s unfinished thought hung between them, unspoken yet undeniably present. A seed of doubt—not yet fully formed, but there all the same.
Hours passed, though neither Mary nor Jane found any true rest. The soft glow of the room remained unchanged, casting shifting patterns over the walls as the stillness stretched between them.

Mary remained seated on the edge of the bed, her fingers absently tracing the grooves of her pendant, as if searching for clarity in its worn surface. Jane had moved to the far side of the room, leaning against the wall with arms folded tight across her chest. Neither had spoken since their last exchange, but the weight of their thoughts filled the space between them.

Jane eventually let out a frustrated sigh, shifting her stance. “He hasn’t come back.”
